# CS 330 Computational Graphics and Visualization

This repository contains my final project for CS 330, Computational Graphics and Visualization. The project demonstrates my work with C++, OpenGL, 3D modeling, textures, lighting, camera movement, and user interaction. The final scene was developed incrementally throughout the course and represents the skills I gained while learning how to construct and render an interactive 3D environment.

## How do I approach designing software?

When designing software, I prefer to break a larger problem into smaller components that can be developed and tested individually. This project helped me improve my ability to plan a 3D scene using simple shapes and transformations before adding more complex features. I learned how to look at real objects and determine how they could be recreated using basic meshes such as boxes, cylinders, spheres, cones, and planes. I also learned to consider how textures, lighting, positioning, scale, and camera perspective work together as part of the overall design.

For my project, I followed an incremental design process. I started with the basic geometry of the scene and gradually introduced camera controls, textures, materials, lighting, and additional objects. This approach allowed me to concentrate on one part of the scene at a time instead of attempting to build everything at once. I can apply this strategy to future software projects by dividing large requirements into smaller features, testing each feature, and integrating them into the larger application once they are working correctly.

## How do I approach developing programs?

While developing my 3D scene, I became more comfortable working with C++, OpenGL, shaders, transformations, textures, and lighting. One of the most important development strategies I used was testing changes frequently! Instead of making many changes before running the program, I found it more effective to implement a feature, build the project, observe the results, and make adjustments based on what I saw. Huge timer saver.

Iteration played a major role throughout the project. Objects frequently needed adjustments to their position, rotation, scale, texture, or lighting before they looked correct in the completed scene. Each milestone also built upon the previous one, which encouraged me to maintain working code while introducing new functionality. My development approach has evolved to place greater emphasis on modularity, experimentation, testing, and incremental improvement. These strategies made debugging easier and helped me successfully develop the final scene without losing functionality that had already been completed.

## How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?

Computational graphics and visualization have easily expanded my understanding of how mathematics, programming, and visual design can harmonize. Through this course, I gained practical experience with coordinate systems, transformations, camera movement, textures, lighting, shaders, and rendering. These concepts will be useful in future computer science courses because they strengthened my understanding of C++ programming and showed me how mathematical concepts can be applied to create interactive visual applications.

Professionally, these skills can also be applied to areas such as software development, application development, game development, simulation, and other forms of interactive visualization. Creating this project gave me experience turning a visual concept into a functioning program while continuously testing and refining the result. More importantly, it strengthened my ability to approach complex development problems systematically, which is a skill I hope to apply well beyond computational graphics!
